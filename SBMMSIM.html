How This Works

MMR does not hit everyone equally. Players near the average get the full force of every adjustment, because that is where most of the population sits and the system has the most room to shuffle them around. Players at the bottom are shielded so they do not quit. Players at the very top still bounce, but there are so few people up there that their lobbies can only get so much harder, so the swing is squashed. Watch the red line: the 66 player gets whiplash.

Network Topology

Both are dedicated servers. The difference is who gets put in the lobby.

No Matchmaking (Connection Only)

All traffic routes through infrastructure. No host advantage. Server-side validation active. No skill filtering. Anyone can be in your lobby.

Dedicated Server (With Matchmaking)

All traffic routes through infrastructure. No host advantage. Server-side validation active. Matchmaking filters by skill. Lobby composition is controlled.

Data and formulas based on Activision's published whitepapers (2024), the Activision matchmaking patent (US 10,322,351), TrueSkill research by Microsoft Research, and publicly available information from Charlie Olson (former Raven Software, MMR algorithm creator).

This is a simulation for educational purposes. It does not use Activision's proprietary source code.
